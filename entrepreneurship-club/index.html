What the club is built to do

Every founder starts in the same place: an idea, a little conviction, and no room to test either one. That's the gap this club closes. Three things happen here, every single month, for founders at any stage, in any country.

Say the idea out loud

An idea kept in your head only ever gets smaller. Bring the one you haven't told anyone yet into a room built for exactly that, trade notes with founders solving your same problem on the other side of the world, and walk out with a sharper version of what you walked in with.

Pitch to people who've actually built something

Stand up and pitch to founders and operators who've raised money, made payroll, and made the mistakes you're about to make. You leave with feedback specific enough to act on Monday morning — not polite nods.

Back your instincts with global data

Every session is grounded in research from the Global Entrepreneurship Monitor, so the conversation isn't guesswork or gut feel — it's informed by what's actually happening to founders across more than 50 countries right now.

No membership fees, no pitch decks required to attend, no gatekeeping by industry or how far along you are. Just one room, once a month, where the idea you're sitting on finally gets tested.

About Us

The founder's background, and why the club exists.

Founder, Entrepreneurship Club

Roger Orozco

Roger Orozco is the founder of the Entrepreneurship Club, bringing over 25 years of experience across business, construction, real estate, and project leadership to the community. He built the club to give entrepreneurs — first-time founders and seasoned operators alike — a consistent, global space to share ideas, ask for honest feedback, and support one another's growth.

Roger earned his bachelor's degree in Finance and International Business from Old Dominion University and later completed his MBA at Regent University, with a concentration in Entrepreneurship. That combination of finance, global business, and entrepreneurial study shapes how the club approaches feedback: practical, numbers-aware, and focused on what actually moves a business forward.

Beyond the club, Roger has served as a board director for a number of institutions. He currently serves on the Board of Directors for the Better Business Bureau (BBB) serving Virginia and for Coastal Mortgage Company, where he has helped shape strategy, vision, and leadership at the board level. He also serves as Executive of Asset Management for Bishard Homes, a real estate company, where he sets strategic direction for the commercial and residential asset management portfolio and sits on the company's executive team. He is additionally the founder of both Just One Opportunity and Orozco Venture Corporation.

Membership

What's required to join, and how the request process works.

Requirements to join

The club works because everyone in the room takes it seriously. Before you request to join, make sure you can commit to the following:

  • Be 18 years or older and have a genuine interest in entrepreneurship, whether you're a founder, an operator, or still shaping an idea.
  • Complete the membership request form with accurate information about yourself and your venture or interest.
  • Agree to the club's Community Respect Policy and follow it in every session.
  • Make a reasonable effort to attend the monthly session, or give notice when you can't.
  • Come ready to both give and receive feedback — the group only works as a two-way exchange.
  • Treat the club as a community, not a sales channel — no unsolicited pitching of products or services to other members.

Community Values

The Respect Policy every member agrees to when they join.

This club works because people feel safe enough to share a half-formed idea and hear the truth about it. Every member — founder, first-timer, mentor — is expected to hold these standards in every session and every conversation that grows out of it.

Respect every background

Members come from different industries, countries, and stages of business. Treat every idea and every person with the same respect, regardless of experience level.

Critique the idea, not the person

Feedback should be honest and specific. Attack weaknesses in a business plan, never the character or ability of the person presenting it.

Keep it confidential

What's shared in a session — ideas, numbers, personal circumstances — stays within the club unless the member gives explicit permission to share it elsewhere.

Share the floor

One monthly session isn't much time. Keep pitches and comments focused so every member who wants feedback gets a fair chance to be heard.

No harassment or discrimination

Harassment, discriminatory remarks, or predatory behavior toward any member is not tolerated, in sessions or in any follow-up contact between members.

No unsolicited selling

This is a community, not a lead list. Don't use the group or member contact information to pitch products or services without being asked.

How this is enforced: a member who violates this policy will first receive a direct conversation about the behavior. A repeated or serious violation — including harassment, discrimination, or breach of confidentiality — results in removal from the club.
